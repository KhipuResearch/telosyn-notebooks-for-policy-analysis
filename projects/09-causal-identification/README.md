## Difference-in-Differences

**Medicaid Expansion and the Uninsured Rate.**

`C01_medicaid_expansion_did/C01_medicaid_expansion_did.ipynb`

**What it shows**

- State a difference-in-differences estimand, and say which assumption stands between the estimate and a causal reading of it.
- Compute a two-by-two difference-in-differences by hand from four group means, and show that a two-way fixed-effects regression returns the same number.
- Read an event-study plot, and explain why the leads — not the lags — are the argument.
- Run two falsification tests (a joint test of pre-period coefficients and a placebo outcome), and apply a decision rule that was written down before the data were seen.

**Sources** — American Community Survey (acs/acs1)  
13 provenance records committed in `C01_medicaid_expansion_did/provenance/`.

**Limitations**

- The 2014 cohort. The design uses only states that expanded on 1 January 2014, against states that never expanded. That makes the timing single and the regression clean, at a cost: sixteen states are not used here (two that expanded part-way through 2014, fourteen later adopters). Later adopters are handled with staggered-timing methods in C03.
- Age band. The outcome is ages 35–64, not the track's 19–64, because no consistent 19–64 series exists across the 2017 change in ACS age bands. Younger adults, who were also affected by the 2010 dependent-coverage rule, are outside this estimate.
- Measurement error. ACS 1-year state estimates carry margins of error that enter the outcome as noise; the regression does not model them.
