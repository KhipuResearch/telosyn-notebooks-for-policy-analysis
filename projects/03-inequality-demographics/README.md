## Income Inequality

**Which Measure, and Why It Matters.**

`D06_inequality_and_wealth/D06_inequality_and_wealth.ipynb`

**What it shows**

- Compute five inequality measures — Gini, Theil T, Atkinson at two aversion parameters, the p90/p10 ratio, and the top-decile share — from published income brackets.
- Explain which region of the distribution each measure is sensitive to, and why that makes them disagree.
- Quantify the disagreement: rank correlation between measures, and how far individual geographies move between rankings.
- Recognise Lorenz curve crossing — two places with nearly identical Gini coefficients and materially different distributions.

**Sources** — Census ACS; CDC PLACES

**Limitations**

- The top-decile share is not identified for half these geographies, because the open `$200,000+` bracket holds 10% or more of households in 25 of 52. It is excluded from the cross-measure comparison and reported only where identified. A tutorial that ranked states on the unguarded statistic would have published an artifact whose correlation with the open-bracket share is about -0.95.
- All measures inherit the bracket assumptions. Within-bracket uniformity and the assumed open-top mean affect every figure here; the sweep above quantifies how much, per measure.
- Survey uncertainty is not propagated to these measures. D03 develops margin-of-error propagation for a derived percentage. Doing the same for a Gini or a Theil index requires replicate weights the public API does not expose, so the rankings here carry sampling uncertainty that is real and unquantified. Treat small differences as noise.

## Demographics and Migration

**When a Rate Is Not Comparable.**

`D07_demographics_and_migration/D07_demographics_and_migration.ipynb`

**What it shows**

- Derive the identity that makes a crude rate a composition-weighted average of group-specific rates, and verify it numerically on real data.
- Compute a directly age-standardised rate, and check it against the property that defines it — a population standardised to its own composition is unchanged.
- Decompose the difference between two crude rates exactly into a rate component and a composition component (Kitagawa), and read the sign pattern that marks a reversal.
- Search the full set of state pairs for orderings that reverse under standardisation, and filter them by whether either ordering is distinguishable given the published margins of error.

**Sources** — American Community Survey (acs/acs5)  
2 provenance records committed in `D07_demographics_and_migration/provenance/`.

**Limitations**

- ACS 5-year estimates are period estimates spanning 2019–2023. They are not a measurement of any single year, and a state whose conditions changed sharply within the window is described by an average of circumstances that never coexisted.
- The poverty threshold is not geographically adjusted. The federal measure applies the same dollar thresholds nationwide, so a state's rate reflects its cost of living only through incomes, not through what those incomes buy. This is a property of the measure, not of the arithmetic here.
- Margins of error across age groups are treated as independent in the quadrature combination. They are not, since the groups are drawn from the same respondents. Both extremes are computed — independence and perfect positive correlation — and every reversal claim above is reported under each, so no conclusion rests on the assumption without saying so.

## The Gender Earnings Gap

**Which Two Numbers You Divide.**

`D33_gender_earnings_gap/D33_gender_earnings_gap.ipynb`

**What it shows**

- Locate a headline statistic's definitional choice, compute the alternatives the same source publishes, and report what the choice is worth.
- Use the ratio MOE, not the proportion MOE, and know which one a pair of estimates calls for.
- Recognise when arithmetic is suggestive rather than probative — a pooled ratio below both components proves composition for means and proves nothing for medians — and go and find the table that settles it.
- See that a conditioning variable can be part of what is being measured.

**Sources** — Census ACS; CDC PLACES

**Limitations**

- A ratio of medians is not a median of ratios, and it is not any individual's experience. It compares two summary statistics of two different distributions and says nothing about the shape of either.
- None of this is a pay-discrimination estimate. No occupation, industry, hours beyond the full-time year-round flag, experience, education or tenure is adjusted for. The notebook is about what the published number is, not about what causes it.
- Section 5 establishes composition, not mechanism. It shows women are less often in the full-time year-round group. It says nothing about why, and the reasons — which include constraint and choice in unmeasured proportions — are exactly what the table cannot see.
