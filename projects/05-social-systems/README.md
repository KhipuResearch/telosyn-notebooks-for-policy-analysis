## Social Mobility

**A Measurement of a Place That No Longer Exists.**

`D10_social_mobility/D10_social_mobility.ipynb`

**What it shows**

- Separate a release date from a reference period when the reference period is a cohort's childhood rather than a year.
- Measure who disclosure protection removes, and notice when the removal is patterned rather than random.
- Read a count that cannot be a count as evidence of what was done to the data.
- Settle whether rank failure is about precision by testing it where precision is good.

**Sources** — American Community Survey (acs/acs5); Opportunity Atlas outcomes, county level  
2 provenance records committed in `D10_social_mobility/provenance/`.

**Limitations**

- One state, one cohort. Virginia's 133 counties, children born 1978–1983. There is no later cohort at this geography in this file, so nothing here speaks to whether mobility has changed.
- The suppression bias is measured for its extent, not corrected. §2 reports how many counties are lost and notes the subset is non-random; it does not reweight, and exercise 2 asks only for a characterisation rather than a fix.
- The noise is acknowledged, not modelled. The published standard errors do not obviously incorporate the injected noise, and this build did not establish whether they do. Every interval in §4 inherits that uncertainty about its own uncertainty.

## Social Services

**A Cross-Tab That Disproves Its Own Inference.**

`D18_social_services/D18_social_services.ipynb`

**What it shows**

- Check whether a cross-tab's second dimension is the criterion you think it is, before reading a rate off it.
- Use a table's own off-diagonal cells as a test of your interpretation.
- Distinguish a programme's eligibility rule from a statistical threshold that shares its name and its base.
- Report a margin on the share you would actually quote, and say which independence assumption it rests on.

**Sources** — Census ACS

**Limitations**

- This notebook does not measure SNAP take-up. It establishes that this table cannot, and proposes no substitute. A real take-up estimate needs an eligibility model over microdata, which ACS summary tables cannot supply.
- "Received SNAP in the past 12 months" is not current enrolment. A household enrolled for one month in the period is counted identically to one enrolled throughout.
- Poverty status in ACS is measured over the same twelve months, so both dimensions are annual and neither is a point-in-time state. A household can be below poverty in the months it was not enrolled and above it in the months it was.

## Device Access

**Two Partitions in One Flat Table.**

`D20_device_access/D20_device_access.ipynb`

**What it shows**

- Read variable labels before summing anything, and recognise when one flat list contains two different partitions.
- Test every identity a table implies, not only the one you expect to hold.
- Quantify a double-count rather than noting that one exists.
- Distinguish a subscription from a capability, and see that a strong correlation between them does not make them interchangeable.

**Sources** — Census ACS; CDC PLACES; CDC NCHS

**Limitations**

- Ownership is not access, and access is not use. The table records what a household reports having. It says nothing about whether the device works, is shared among several people, or is used.
- "Smartphone-only" is a device statement, not a capability measurement. The inference that such households can do less is reasonable and is not measured anywhere here.
- The overlapping rows are correct and only their sum is wrong. Nothing in section 4 is a criticism of the Census Bureau; the table answers a multiple-response question and reports it accordingly.

## Social Capital

**The Best-Measured Quantity Here, and It Still Cannot Be Ranked.**

`D21_social_capital/D21_social_capital.ipynb`

**What it shows**

- Distinguish two kinds of absence — a unit removed before publication, and a unit present with an empty estimate — and know why a file can only ever show you one of them.
- Read a platform universe as something other than a sample of a population.
- Recognise uncertainty that is partly a policy parameter, and test whether its components can be separated from what the publisher released.
- Settle §7.2 by testing it where precision is not the constraint.

**Sources** — Census ACS; CDC PLACES; Opportunity Atlas; Social Capital Atlas

**Limitations**

- One snapshot, one release. The network was observed on 28 May 2022 and there is no second vintage. Nothing here speaks to change over time, and no revision is possible to detect.
- The scaling test is reported as a failure, not a result. §2 establishes that the components cannot be separated from published columns. It does not establish the true scaling of either component, and the slopes should not be quoted as estimates of anything.
- Reliability is computed from the published standard error, which contains the privacy noise. So the reliability figure is itself partly a function of ε, and a different budget would give a different λ for the same network. §3's conclusion is unaffected — a *larger* λ would not create determined ranks — but the specific value is not a pure property of the measurement.

## Innovation and Entrepreneurship

**Three Reasons the Parts Do Not Sum to the Whole.**

`D29_innovation_and_entrepreneurship/D29_business_dynamics.ipynb`

**What it shows**

- Distinguish three failure modes of aggregation — definitional double counting, coverage exclusion, and injected noise — and tell them apart from the data alone.
- Read a disclosure-avoidance method as policy, with an issue date and a successor, rather than as a fixed characteristic of a dataset.
- Build a regime detector from the digit distribution of published values.
- Recognise a withheld cell that arrives as the number zero, and see what asking for the flag column changes and what it does not.

**Sources** — BLS QCEW; Census CBP; Census BDS

**Limitations**

- One reference year. Every measurement is 2022. The three mechanisms are structural and should be stable, but their magnitudes are not measured across years here, and the statewide-records deficit in particular could move with the size of the leasing industry.
- The statewide (999) records are inferred, never observed. The API does not serve them. §4 establishes that the deficit behaves exactly as the codebook's description predicts, and that a suppression-only explanation does not fit the sector pattern. It does not observe a single statewide record, and a reader who needs the actual figure must go to the published flat files rather than the API.
- The sector attribution is a pattern match, not a decomposition. The employment gap in each sector is a residual containing both mechanisms. The ratio separates which one dominates; it does not apportion the gap between them.
