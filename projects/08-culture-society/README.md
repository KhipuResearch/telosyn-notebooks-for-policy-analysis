## Cultural Economics

**The Number Is the Boundary.**

`D11_cultural_economics/D11_cultural_economics.ipynb`

**What it shows**

- Recognise a satellite account and understand why its perfect internal consistency is a property of accounting rather than evidence of accuracy.
- Locate a statistic's error budget when no uncertainty is published, by varying the definition instead of the data.
- Refuse to sum a column whose rows are a grand total, a subject total, subtotals and leaves with no depth marker between them.
- Test a ranking against its own construct rather than against sampling noise, which is the only test available when there is no sampling noise.

**Sources** — BLS LAUS; BEA; FRED / ALFRED; CDC PLACES

**Limitations**

- One year. Every cross-sectional figure is 2023. §4 shows the national share across five years; the boundary-sensitivity result in §3 is not recomputed for other years, and nothing here establishes that the rank movements are stable over time.
- The alternative boundaries are illustrative, not authoritative. "Core arts only" is BEA's own line 100, and "less the largest three" is a choice made in this notebook and named in the configuration cell. Neither is a better definition than BEA's. They exist to measure sensitivity, and a different pair would give different numbers.
- No independent benchmark. This notebook tests the account against variations of itself. It does not compare ACPSA against an external count of arts organisations, employment or receipts, so it can show that the answer depends on the boundary without showing which boundary is closer to anything.

## Cultural Economics

**A Sector You Cannot Size.**

`D22_nonprofit_arts/D22_nonprofit_arts.ipynb`

**What it shows**

- Check classification coverage before computing a sector total, and treat a coding gap as a sampling problem rather than a data-cleaning one.
- Test whether missingness is random against a variable you have, rather than assuming it.
- Bound a quantity under stated assumptions, and report the width of the bound as the result.
- Recognise when the honest output is "this cannot be computed here", and say what would be needed instead.

**Sources** — BLS QCEW; BEA; IRS BMF

**Limitations**

- This notebook produces no estimate of the nonprofit arts sector. That is the finding, not an omission.
- `REVENUE_AMT` is not a common year. It is the most recent period on file per organisation, so every dollar total here sums across mixed fiscal years and is not a time-consistent aggregate.
- Revenue is absent for 29.2% of rows for reasons unrelated to NTEE, including filing thresholds. Sections 6 and 7's dollar figures are over organisations that reported, which is a second non-random subset stacked on the first.

## Gentrification

**Measuring a Concept the Data Cannot Resolve.**

`D27_housing_affordability_and_gentrification/D27_gentrification.ipynb`

**What it shows**

- Separate a construct from its operationalisation, and recognise when a dataset's geographic grain cannot resolve the process being named.
- Trace a coverage cascade end to end, and weight it two ways — by unit and by population — because those give opposite impressions of the same sample.
- Deflate a long nominal series, and establish how much of a headline change is price level rather than relative movement.
- Justify a deflator choice: why CPI excluding shelter is the defensible one for housing, and what deflation can and cannot change about a ranking.

**Sources** — American Community Survey (acs/acs5/profile); American Community Survey (acs/acs5); FRED series CPIAUCSL; FRED series CUUR0000SA0L2; ZHVI, All Homes (SFR/Condo), smoothed & seasonally adjusted, county (2026-08 release (series through 2026-07))  
7 provenance records committed in `D27_housing_affordability_and_gentrification/provenance/`.

**Limitations**

- The unit of analysis does not match the construct. Counties cannot resolve a neighbourhood process. Everything below is conditioned on that and no result here should be described as a gentrification measure.
- The sample is selected, not random. 32% of counties, holding 72% of occupied units. Counties without a 2000 ZHVI value are systematically smaller and more rural, and that is correlated with housing-market behaviour.
- ZHVI is a modelled index, not transaction prices. It is produced from Zillow's own valuation model, revised historically, and its methodology changed materially in 2023 with the neural Zestimate. A 2000–2026 series is therefore not a constant instrument.

## Rent Burden

**Three Ways to Get a Defensible-Looking Number Wrong.**

`D27_housing_affordability_and_gentrification/D27_rent_burden.ipynb`

**What it shows**

- Identify which published rows belong to the distribution being measured and which belong to the universe but not the distribution, and quantify what dividing by the wrong base costs.
- Propagate margins of error through a derived denominator, recognising that a base you construct has a margin you must compute rather than inherit.
- Settle the denominator question against an external figure published by the Bureau itself, rather than by argument.
- Distinguish a ratio of medians from the median of a ratio, and measure the disagreement on real data under both a correct and an incorrect income universe.

**Sources** — American Community Survey (acs/acs5/profile); American Community Survey (acs/acs5); FRED series CPIAUCSL; FRED series CUUR0000SA0L2; ZHVI, All Homes (SFR/Condo), smoothed & seasonally adjusted, county (2026-08 release (series through 2026-07))  
7 provenance records committed in `D27_housing_affordability_and_gentrification/provenance/`.

**Limitations**

- ACS 5-year estimates are period estimates spanning 2019–2023, a window containing extraordinary rental-market movement and temporary federal rental assistance. The figures describe an average of that period and no single year within it.
- Gross rent includes utilities where the tenant pays them, so two households paying the same contract rent can record different gross rents. Cross-area comparison inherits that.
- The 30 per cent threshold is a convention with a known critique. It applies the same ratio at every income level, so it classifies a high-income household with high rent as burdened and can miss a low-income household below the ratio who has nothing left after rent. Residual-income measures address this and are not computable from this table.
