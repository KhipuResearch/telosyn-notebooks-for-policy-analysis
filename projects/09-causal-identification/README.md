## Difference-in-Differences

**Medicaid Expansion and the Uninsured Rate.**

`C01_medicaid_expansion_did/C01_medicaid_expansion_did.ipynb`

**What it shows**

- State a difference-in-differences estimand, and say which assumption stands between the estimate and a causal reading of it.
- Compute a two-by-two difference-in-differences by hand from four group means, and show that a two-way fixed-effects regression returns the same number.
- Read an event-study plot, and explain why the leads — not the lags — are the argument.
- Run two falsification tests (a joint test of pre-period coefficients and a placebo outcome), and apply a decision rule that was written down before the data were seen.

**Sources** — American Community Survey (acs/acs1)  
13 provenance records committed in `C01_medicaid_expansion_did/provenance/`.

**Limitations**

- The 2014 cohort. The design uses only states that expanded on 1 January 2014, against states that never expanded. That makes the timing single and the regression clean, at a cost: sixteen states are not used here (two that expanded part-way through 2014, fourteen later adopters). Later adopters are handled with staggered-timing methods in C03.
- Age band. The outcome is ages 35–64, not the track's 19–64, because no consistent 19–64 series exists across the 2017 change in ACS age bands. Younger adults, who were also affected by the 2010 dependent-coverage rule, are outside this estimate.
- Measurement error. ACS 1-year state estimates carry margins of error that enter the outcome as noise; the regression does not model them.

## Regression Discontinuity

**Health Insurance Coverage at Age 65.**

`C02_medicare_age65_rdd/C02_medicare_age65_rdd.ipynb`

**What it shows**

- Explain why a threshold rule such as an eligibility age behaves like an experiment, and what has to be true for that to hold.
- Estimate a jump at a threshold with a local-linear fit and a triangular kernel, and show the estimate against the binned means it summarises.
- Report how the answer moves with the bandwidth instead of presenting one bandwidth as the answer.
- Run three falsification tests — a balance check on the running variable, placebo cutoffs and covariate continuity — and apply a decision rule written before the data were seen.

**Sources** — American Community Survey Public Use Microdata Sample (acs/acs1/pums)  
153 provenance records committed in `C02_medicare_age65_rdd/provenance/`.

**Limitations**

- Local to 65. The estimate is the jump at one threshold. It says nothing about the effect of coverage at any other age, or about the people far from 65.
- A discrete running variable. Age is reported in whole years, so the nearest observations are a year apart and the fit rests on a handful of age cells per side. Intervals use the heteroskedasticity-robust variance over those cells; they are not corrected for the discreteness, and there is no bias-corrected estimator here (`rdrobust` is not adopted).
- The kernel drops the end ages. A triangular kernel gives zero weight at the edge of the window, so a bandwidth of *k* years uses *k* − 1 ages on each side: the headline *k* = 5 fits four ages per side, which is what the `n_left` and `n_right` columns show. That follows from the plan's kernel and bandwidth definitions and is not a departure from them.

**Limitations**

- Suppressed county cells. The balanced-panel rule drops every county with a withheld or zero cell in any quarter, and withholding is not random: it is applied where few establishments make a cell identifying. The retrieval and panel cells print how many counties and how much employment that costs; the estimates speak to the counties that remain.
- Window. The BLS slice API starts in 2014 Q1, so no state has a pre-period before 2014 and the states already above the federal minimum in 2014 have none at all. They are in the naive regression and its decomposition and absent from the interaction-weighted estimate, which therefore speaks only to adopters from 2015 to 2023.
- Dating. The schedule records January-1 values, so an increase made part-way through a year is dated to the next 1 January: up to two quarters of treatment are coded as untreated.

## Synthetic Control

**California SB 3 and Leisure-and-Hospitality Employment.**

`C04_ca_minimum_wage_synth/C04_ca_minimum_wage_synth.ipynb`

**What it shows**

**Limitations**

- Permutation inference depends on the pool. With 21 donors the smallest possible p-value is 1/22, and the p-value is valid only for the pool as built. A different donor rule would give a different distribution.
- Convex hull. California is much larger than any donor; a weighted average of smaller states may not be able to reach it. The pre-period fit says how well it did.
- Anticipation. SB 3 was chaptered in April 2016, nine months before the first increase. Employers may have responded in the last months of the pre-period, which the pre-period fit would not reveal.

**Public Coverage and Employment.**

**What it shows**

**Sources** — American Community Survey Public Use Microdata Sample (acs/acs1/pums)  
2 provenance records committed in `C05_public_coverage_matching/provenance/`.

**Limitations**

- The assumption cannot be tested. No unmeasured confounding is the whole claim, and public coverage is plausibly conferred on grounds that bear on work: disability, caregiving, health, state rules. The negative control and the Rosenbaum Gamma say something about it; neither proves it.
- State. State is not in the cells; the state's Medicaid expansion status stands in for it. Other state differences are omitted and are exactly the kind of hidden bias Gamma describes.
- What `PUBCOV` is. It mixes programmes with different rules, and includes Medicare and military coverage. The treated group is not one intervention.

## Instrumental Variables

**Medicaid Expansion as an Instrument for Coverage.**

`C06_medicaid_expansion_iv/C06_medicaid_expansion_iv.ipynb`

**What it shows**

- Explain what an instrument must do (move the treatment) and must not do (move the outcome any other way), and which of the two can be checked.
- Estimate a two-stage least squares effect on grouped cells with population weights and state-clustered errors, and read the first stage, the reduced form and the ratio between them.
- Say who the estimate is about: the compliers whose coverage changed because their state expanded, and how large that group is.
- Measure what grouping and weighting do to the estimand, by comparing the weighted and equal-weight estimates.

**Sources** — American Community Survey Public Use Microdata Sample (acs/acs1/pums)  
280 provenance records committed in `C06_medicaid_expansion_iv/provenance/`.

**Limitations**

- A complier effect. The estimate belongs to the people whose Medicaid coverage changed because their state expanded, in the treated states and in this window. It says nothing about people whose coverage does not respond to expansion, and the notebook prints how large the responding group is (the first stage).
- The exclusion restriction is an argument, not a test. Expansion may change employment by routes other than coverage: state labour demand in health care, other state policies adopted alongside expansion, changes in how coverage is reported when people move from other coverage to Medicaid, and, in later years, work requirements in some states. Only the effect of health and of loosened "job lock" through coverage is the effect of interest; the estimate is the most the argument can support.
- Timing. Only the 2014 cohort and the never-adopters are used, so later adopters are excluded, and some treated states broadened coverage before 2014.
