## Subgroup Effects and Multiple Comparisons

**C01's Design Run Seven Times.**

`Q01_subgroup_effects_multiplicity/Q01_subgroup_effects_multiplicity.ipynb`

**What it shows**

- Run one difference-in-differences design in several subgroups, and say what each subgroup estimate is about.
- Count how many subgroup effects look distinguishable from zero before and after a multiple-comparison correction, with Holm's procedure (family-wise error) and Benjamini–Hochberg (false discovery rate), and say what each one controls.
- State the power of each subgroup test: the smallest effect it could detect, and the chance it would detect an effect as large as the all-adult estimate.
- Read a subgroup that does not clear the bar as *unable to say*, not as *no effect*, and see why "this subgroup is distinguishable from zero and that one is not" is not a statement about a difference between them.

**Sources** — American Community Survey (acs/acs1); KFF Medicaid expansion status  
10 provenance records committed in `Q01_subgroup_effects_multiplicity/provenance/`.

**Limitations**

- The departure from the specification. "The specification" is the project brief for this notebook, which fixed the data tables before the pre-analysis plan was written; the brief is not part of this repository, and the plan (`Q01_pap.md`) records what was done. The specification named the ACS subject table S2701 for the subgroups. This notebook uses the ACS detail tables B27001 and its race iterations instead, so its subgroups are race and ethnicity and three age bands inside 35–64, not S2701's broader age bands. The reasons are in the Data Provenance note. Age bands outside 35–64 are not examined.
- C01's estimate is borderline, and every subgroup inherits that. The reference interval's upper end is a hair from zero and its verdict moves with the number of bootstrap draws (printed in the notebook from C01's stored output). A subgroup result can be no firmer than the average it splits.
- Power. The notebook prints each subgroup's minimum detectable effect and its power to detect an effect as large as C01's, for an uncorrected two-sided 5% test and for the first step of Holm's procedure, which is the threshold the smallest p-value must clear and is where the survivor's power is read. Where the power is low, a subgroup that does not clear the bar says the test could not tell; it does not say the effect is absent.

## Bayesian Partial Pooling of County Poverty Estimates

**What the Model Adds and What the Prior Adds.**

`Q03_bayesian_pooling_county_estimates/Q03_bayesian_pooling_county_estimates.ipynb`

**What it shows**

- Write the normal-normal hierarchical model for estimates with known standard errors, and say what its shrinkage factor is.
- Convert two ACS margins of error into the standard error of a proportion.
- Check a sampler three ways: convergence diagnostics, an exact calculation that needs no sampling, and a posterior predictive coverage check.
- Show a prior's contribution by refitting under other priors, and report which counties' estimates are mostly the prior.

**Sources** — American Community Survey (acs/acs5)  
1 provenance record committed in `Q03_bayesian_pooling_county_estimates/provenance/`.

**Limitations**

- Departure from the pre-analysis plan. The plan was not edited. Its first fit (2000 retained draws per chain) stopped under its own stop rule: R-hat 1.0060 and no divergence, but a smallest effective sample size of 305 against the floor of 400. No number from that fit was read. Amendment 1, committed after the retrieval and before the rerun, raised the retained draws to 8000 for every fit and changed nothing else; the stop is on the run ledger. Two further readings are stated here: the plan asks for R-hat and effective sample sizes "for every place", and the notebook prints the largest R-hat and the smallest effective sample sizes over `mu`, `tau` and all 133 places, which bound every one of them; and the Census variable labels in the request are D04's own labels.
- The margins of error are taken as known and normal. They are themselves estimates, and the 90% margins are converted to standard errors by dividing by 1.645.
- The rate-scale model ignores that a rate lies between zero and one. The exploratory logit-scale refit moves 9 of 133 places by more than one percentage point, the largest being Radford city, which is among the highest-poverty places; the normal form on the rate scale is a modelling choice with consequences at the top of the distribution.
