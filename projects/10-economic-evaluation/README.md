## Cost-Effectiveness

**Spending and Coverage Gained in the 2014 Medicaid Expansion.**

`E01_cost_per_newly_insured/E01_cost_per_newly_insured.ipynb`

**What it shows**

- Define a cost-effectiveness ratio as cost per unit of effect against a comparator, and say what the comparator is here (no expansion).
- Build the numerator from a government expenditure file, with its negative adjustments, blanks and zeros read as they are and not repaired.
- Build the denominator from a causal estimate, and see why the population the cost covers and the population the effect covers have to be the same.
- Propagate uncertainty from the effect and from a range of costs into a ratio, and read a cost-effectiveness acceptability curve.

**Sources** — American Community Survey Public Use Microdata Sample (acs/acs1/pums); State FIPS codes and names; CMS Medicaid CMS-64; KFF Medicaid expansion status  
281 provenance records committed in `E01_cost_per_newly_insured/provenance/`.

**Limitations**

- The ratio is not estimable with useful precision. The effect's 95% interval includes zero, so the cost per newly insured person-year has no finite upper limit; the ratio set printed above gives only a floor. The point ratio is arithmetic and is not a finding. Spending and the effect are reported separately for that reason.
- The perspective is the public payer, on a cash basis, gross. The cost is gross public spending in the CMS file, summed as reported. Prior-period adjustments reported after 2019 Q4 are outside the window, and adjustments inside it may relate to earlier quarters. Offsets that would lower the net cost are omitted (reduced uncompensated care, state savings, tax and employment effects), as is administrative cost, which would raise it. The marketplace premium tax credits that the expansion displaced or did not are in neither the numerator nor the effect.
- The plan's numerator is not incremental spending. The VIII Group total computable includes spending on adults who were covered before 2014 under waivers, now paid at a different federal match; the numerator cell prints how much, and for which states. Newly eligible spending is the closer measure of incremental spending and is reported beside the plan's figure, as a departure after review. Crowd-out of other coverage raises the numerator without raising the gain; woodwork enrollees (eligible before 2014, enrolling after) are paid under regular categories, so are missing from the numerator and present in the gain. Their directions differ, and none is netted out.

## Distributional Incidence

**Who Pays Federal Taxes, Who Gets Transfers, and Who Would Get a Child Transfer.**

`E03_distributional_incidence/E03_distributional_incidence.ipynb`

**What it shows**

- Read a distributional table (average income, transfers and taxes by income quintile) and turn averages into shares of a total.
- Rank people by household income adjusted for household size, and place a boundary inside a bin when income is known only to its bin.
- Compute the incidence of a stylised transfer by income quintile, race and ethnicity, and the age of the household's reference person.
- Report a survey share with the interval a simple random sample would give, and say why that interval likely understates the true uncertainty.

**Sources** — The Distribution of Household Income, 2023 - supplemental data (September 2026 report (publication 62761)); Current Population Survey Annual Social and Economic Supplement (cps/asec/mar); CPS ASEC 2024 replicate-weight download: 2024_ASEC_Replicate_Weight_Usage_Instructions.docx; CPS ASEC 2024 replicate-weight download: CPS_ASEC_ASCII_REPWGT_2024.SAS; CPS ASEC 2024 replicate-weight download: CPS_ASEC_ASCII_REPWGT_2024.ZIP  
11 provenance records committed in `E03_distributional_incidence/provenance/`.

**Limitations**

- Plan and amendments. The plan was committed before any retrieval and never edited. Amendment 1 (before the first CPS retrieval) set the bins, tails, floor and a 1% withheld-weight bound. The first CPS retrieval exceeded that bound and stopped; amendment 2 (after that retrieval, on the owner's ruling, before any incidence was computed) moved the bound to 2% and required every share to be bounded by the withheld weight, so the bound was chosen after the figure was seen. The configuration cell prints each file's commit, author time and digest: | File | Role | Relation to the first CPS retrieval | |---|---|---| | `E03_plan.md` | the plan | before | | `E03_plan_amendment_1.md` | bins, tails, floor, 1% bound | before | | `E03_plan_amendment_2.md` | bound moved to 2%, shares bounded by the withheld weight | after | The stored provenance is a re-run whose counts match the first retrieval; the first retrieval's record and a run ledger were reconstructed afterwards and are marked as such.
- The two income concepts differ and are not harmonised. The table in the data section sets out what each includes. The CPS boundaries sit below CBO's and the CPS puts fewer children in its lowest quintile; the notebook describes that as definition plus measurement without apportioning it. The plan expected to compare mean incomes by quintile; the CBO workbook publishes no size-adjusted mean, so the notebook compares boundaries and counts. That departure was decided on seeing the file and is labelled "not pre-specified (added after review, 2026-10-28)" in its cell.
- CBO's tax and transfer figures are modelled allocations, not a count of who writes the cheque: the corporate income tax is split between capital and labour, employers' payroll taxes are charged to workers and Medicaid is valued at cost to the government. The shares of the stylised transfer are estimates of where children are, multiplied by a constant; there is no take-up, phase-out, taxation or behavioural response in the body.
