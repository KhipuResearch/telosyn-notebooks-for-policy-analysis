## Crime and Public Safety

**When the Coverage Is the Variable.**

`D13_crime_and_public_safety/D13_crime_and_public_safety.ipynb`

**What it shows**

- Read a participation series as data, not as metadata, and notice when the publisher has filed it somewhere that invites you not to.
- Verify which denominator a published rate uses rather than inferring it from the name.
- Distinguish an outage from an absence, when the upstream returns a failure that a careless client turns into a zero.
- Recognise a coverage figure that is true and useless, and say precisely what it does and does not certify.

**Sources** — BLS QCEW; CDC PLACES; FBI CDE

**Limitations**

- Ten states, not fifty. The national picture would need 51 live calls against an upstream that fails intermittently, which is not a reproducible artifact. Every figure here describes the named states and nothing beyond them. The states were chosen to span the participation range, which means they are not a sample and no statistic here has a standard error.
- One offence type. Violent crime only. Whether coverage behaves identically for property offences is exercise 3, not a result.
- The correlation in §4 has n = 10, on states named rather than sampled. It is reported to establish that no usable coefficient is available here, not to estimate one: it carries no standard error and a different ten states would give a different number. §4 also applies §3's data-quality screen before recomputing, because a state whose monthly counts are not a measurement cannot contribute a meaningful rate change. The first version of this notebook left it in, and would have used a figure it had just finished disqualifying.

## Civic Engagement

**When There Is No Sampling Error At All.**

`D19_civic_engagement/D19_civic_engagement.ipynb`

**What it shows**

- Read a source that has no uncertainty statement because none exists, and separate that from a source that has one and withholds it.
- Test an accounting identity before and after cleaning, and treat a worsening as evidence about your assumption rather than about the data.
- Recognise a column whose meaning is not constant across units, and stop before aggregating it.
- Detect two vintages inside one download, and know why a lexical sort of a version column is not a chronological one.

**Sources** — BLS LAUS; BEA; CDC PLACES; MIT Election Lab; Zillow

**Limitations**

- Zero sampling error is not zero error. Votes are miscounted, recounted, challenged and certified late. This file records certified returns; it does not verify them, and nothing in it would show a counting failure.
- The identity test bounds internal consistency, not accuracy. A state whose parts sum to its published total can still be wrong in both.
- `is_ballot_disposition` is a declared list, not a detector. Six labels are named in `ELECTION_BALLOT_DISPOSITIONS`, five of which appear in this vintage. A new label in a future release would not be flagged, and there is no way to tell a person from a disposition programmatically.

## Transportation Equity

**A Series That Is Not a Partition.**

`D23_transportation_equity/D23_transportation_equity.ipynb`

**What it shows**

- Test whether a set of categories partitions its universe before treating it as one, using a check that costs one line.
- Distinguish a subset from a sibling among categories presented identically.
- Notice a universe narrower than the obvious denominator, and identify who it drops.
- Report a group comparison without a rate you cannot construct.

**Sources** — American Community Survey (acs/acs5)  
1 provenance record committed in `D23_transportation_equity/provenance/`.

**Limitations**

- One state, one vintage. The partition structure is a property of the ACS and holds everywhere; the magnitudes are Virginia's 2019–2023.
- Point estimates. MOEs are retrieved and pinned in provenance but not propagated. Iterations `C` and `E` are small enough that their shares carry wide intervals, and exercise 4 asks for them.
- The group-quarters bias is argued, not measured. That those workers have lower vehicle access is a reasonable expectation given who lives in barracks and dormitories; this source contains no information about them at all, so the direction is asserted and the magnitude is unknown. Exercise 3 bounds it under assumptions rather than resolving it.

## Crime Aggregates

**When the Arithmetic Is Perfect and the Number Is Useless.**

`D26_crime_aggregates/D26_crime_aggregates.ipynb`

**What it shows**

- Decompose an aggregate before quoting its movement, and report the component spread beside it.
- Distinguish an aggregation problem from an accounting problem. A total that sums correctly can still be the wrong thing to report.
- Count the tests you ran, not the ones that survived, and compute the family-wise rate.
- Weigh a significant result against the mechanism it implies, and say when the mechanism argues against it.

**Sources** — FBI CDE

**Limitations**

- Everything D13 says about this source applies here — voluntary reporting, a changing agency set, and rates normalised by covered population. None of it is repeated and none of it is resolved.
- Fifteen named states, not a sample. The medians are medians over those fifteen and are not national estimates. No weighting by population is applied.
- Two endpoints of a period, not a trend. 2019 against 2023 says nothing about the path between them, which for several of these offences was not monotonic.
